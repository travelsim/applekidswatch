## 2. Choose GPS+Cellular (Not Just GPS)

This sounds counterintuitive — cellular adds cost, right?

But here's the thing: **Family Setup requires cellular**. Without it, your child's watch can't make calls, send iMessages, or share their location when away from Wi-Fi.

The GPS-only model is cheaper upfront ($249 new vs $299 for cellular), but it doesn't work as a kids' communication device. You'd need your child to carry an iPhone too — which defeats the purpose.

**The real saving:** A refurbished GPS+Cellular SE at $199 is still **$100 less** than a new GPS-only model, and actually works for what you need.

## 4. Buy Last Year's Generation

The Apple Watch SE 2 (2023) and SE 3 (2025) share almost the same feature set. The SE 3 has a slightly faster processor (S9 vs S8), but for a child using messaging, GPS, and Schooltime mode — the SE 2 is more than enough.

**Savings:** An SE 2 refurbished costs **$30–$50 less** than an SE 3 refurbished. In terms of real-world performance for a kid, you won't notice the difference.

The one thing to watch: Apple may stop supporting SE 2 with watchOS updates in 2–3 years. For a device your child will use for 2 years and pass down, that's fine. For a "buy it for the next 4 years" purchase, spring for the SE 3.

## 7. Add an Extended Warranty (Yes, It Saves Money)

This sounds like a cost, not a saving. But hear us out.

If your child breaks their watch — and statistically, about 5–10% of kids damage their device within the first year — you're looking at:

- **Apple out-of-warranty repair:** $199+ (they often just replace the whole watch)
- **New replacement watch:** $229–$299
- **Extended warranty ($19.99):** Accidental damage covered

Think of it as insurance. One drop on a tile floor and the extended warranty has paid for itself 10 times over.

**The math:** If there's a 10% chance your child breaks the watch (conservative estimate for a 7-year-old), the expected value of the warranty is:
- 10% × $200 (repair cost) = $20
- Warranty cost: $19.99

Breakeven. And if you have a particularly accident-prone kid, it's a no-brainer.
