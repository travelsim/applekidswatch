So you got your child an Apple Watch. Now what?

Family Setup does a lot of the heavy lifting, but to get the most out of it — and to ensure your child stays safe, focused, and connected — you'll want to tweak a few key settings. Here's your complete walkthrough, starting with the most important ones.

---

## Before You Begin: How to Access Family Settings

All of your child's watch settings are controlled from **your iPhone**, not from the watch itself. Open the **Watch app** on your iPhone, tap **All Watches** at the top left, then tap the ⓘ icon next to your child's watch. That's your control center.

---

## 1. Approved Contacts (The Most Important Setting)

**Why it matters:** By default, your child can only call and text people you approve. No strangers. No spam calls. No group chats from classmates they barely know.

**How to set it up:**
1. In the Watch app, tap your child's watch → **Contacts**
2. Tap **Add Contact** — choose from your own contacts
3. Add parents, grandparents, a trusted neighbor, and maybe one or two close friends
4. For each contact, you can choose: allow calls only, messages only, or both

**Pro tip:** Add yourself, your partner, and at least one backup contact (grandparent or neighbor) who could help in an emergency. If you're unreachable, they're the safety net.

---

## 2. Schooltime Mode (Focus During Class)

**Why it matters:** Schooltime Mode turns the watch into a basic timepiece during school hours. No games. No messages. No distractions. A distinctive yellow ring appears on the screen so teachers know it's in school mode.

**How to set it up:**
1. In the Watch app → your child's watch → **Schooltime**
2. Tap **Schedule** → set the hours your child is in school (e.g., 8:00 AM – 3:00 PM)
3. Choose which days of the week apply
4. Toggle **Block During Schooltime** to prevent them from exiting the mode

During Schooltime, the watch only shows the time. Your child can still make emergency calls. You'll still get their location updates.

**Pro tip:** Set up a separate schedule for after-school activities. If they have tutoring on Tuesdays at 4 PM, add a second Schooltime block.

---

## 3. Find My: Location Sharing & Geofencing

**Why it matters:** This is the feature that gives you peace of mind. You can see exactly where your child is in real-time, and get notified when they arrive or leave specific locations.

**How to set it up:**
1. On your iPhone, open the **Find My** app
2. Tap the **Devices** tab
3. Select your child's Apple Watch
4. Location sharing should be ON by default (if not, tap "Share My Location")

**To set up Arrival/Departure alerts (geofencing):**
1. In Find My → Devices → your child's watch
2. Tap **Add** under Notifications
3. Choose **Notify Me** → **Arrival** or **Departure**
4. Select a location (school, home, soccer field) and set the radius

You'll get a push notification when they arrive at school or leave practice. No checking required.

---

## 4. Emergency SOS (Safety Net)

**Why it matters:** If your child ever feels unsafe, they can hold the side button for two seconds. The watch calls 911 (or your local emergency number), sends your emergency contacts a message with their location, and displays your child's Medical ID to first responders.

**How to set it up:**
1. In the Watch app → your child's watch → **Emergency SOS**
2. Toggle **Hold Side Button to Call** to ON
3. Tap **Set Up Emergency Contacts** in the Health app
4. Add yourself and at least one other trusted contact

**Pro tip:** Practice with your child. Have them hold the side button until they feel the haptic feedback and see the countdown. Explain that it's only for real emergencies.

---

## 5. Screen Time & App Limits

**Why it matters:** The Apple Watch has games, apps, and messaging — all the dopamine hits of a smartphone, just smaller. Screen Time lets you set boundaries.

**How to set it up:**
1. In the Watch app → your child's watch → **Screen Time**
2. Tap **App Limits** → choose categories or specific apps to limit
3. Set a time limit (e.g., 30 minutes for games per day)
4. Set **Downtime** — a period (like 8 PM – 7 AM) when only essential apps work

**Pro tip:** Be more generous with communication apps (Messages, Phone) and stricter with games and social features. Communication is the reason you bought the watch.

---

## 6. Ask to Buy (Parent Approval for Purchases & Downloads)

**Why it matters:** Without Ask to Buy, your child can download apps, buy songs, or make in-app purchases. With it, every transaction sends a request to your phone.

**How to set it up:**
1. On your iPhone, go to **Settings** → **Family** → your child's name
2. Tap **Ask to Buy**
3. Toggle **Require Purchase Approval** to ON

When your child tries to download an app or make a purchase, you'll get a notification on your iPhone. You can approve or decline instantly.

---

## 7. Apple Cash Family (Allowance With Training Wheels)

**Why it matters:** Want to teach your kid about money? Apple Cash Family lets you send them an allowance that works anywhere Apple Pay is accepted. And you can see every transaction.

**How to set it up:**
1. On your iPhone → **Wallet** app → **Apple Cash**
2. Tap **Set Up Apple Cash Family**
3. Follow the prompts to add your child
4. Send money as needed, or set up recurring weekly allowance

Your child can only spend what you send them. No overdraft. No surprise charges.

---

## 8. Theater Mode & Do Not Disturb (Battery Saver Combo)

**Why it matters:** The Apple Watch's biggest weakness is battery life — about 18 hours with cellular. Theater Mode + Do Not Disturb can stretch that to 24+ hours.

**How to set it up:**
1. On the watch itself, swipe up to open **Control Center**
2. Tap the **masks icon** (Theater Mode) — screen stays off unless tapped
3. Tap the **crescent moon icon** (Do Not Disturb) — silences notifications

**Pro tip:** Enable Theater Mode during school hours (on top of Schooltime) to maximize battery life. The watch still tracks location and can receive calls — the screen just doesn't light up.

---

## 9. Water Lock (For Swimming & Bath Time)

**Why it matters:** The Apple Watch SE is swim-proof to 50 meters, but the touchscreen can act weird when wet. Water Lock disables the screen until you unlock it.

**How to use it:**
1. Swipe up → Control Center → tap the **water drop icon**
2. After swimming, turn the **Digital Crown** to unlock — the watch ejects any water from the speaker

There's nothing to configure. Just teach your child to enable it before swimming.

---

## 10. Walkie-Talkie (The Fun One)

**Why it matters:** Walkie-Talkie is a surprisingly fun, useful feature. Your child can tap a button and send a quick voice message directly to your watch or iPhone. It's quicker than typing and feels like a walkie-talkie.

**How to set it up:**
1. On your child's watch, open the **Walkie-Talkie** app
2. Tap **Add Friend** → select you (or other approved contacts)
3. You'll get an invitation on your iPhone or Apple Watch
4. Accept it, and you're connected

**Pro tip:** Walkie-Talkie is great for quick check-ins. "Where are you?" "Coming!" But it can get annoying if left on all day. Teach your child to turn it off when they don't need it.

## Final Tips for Parents

1. **Don't do it all at once.** Set up the critical settings (Approved Contacts, Schooltime, Emergency SOS) first. Add Screen Time and Apple Cash over the next few days.
2. **Explain the rules to your child.** The watch is a tool, not a toy (okay, it's a little bit of a toy). Set expectations around Schooltime Mode and app usage.
3. **Check in after a week.** Ask your child: What's working? What's confusing? Is the battery lasting? Adjust settings as needed.
4. **You can always change settings remotely.** Don't like how something is working? Open the Watch app and tweak it. Your child won't even know.
